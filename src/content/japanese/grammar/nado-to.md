equivalents:
  - "(saying / thinking) things like ~ — など + quotative と, citing speech or thought, often with disdain or dismissal"
nuance: "などと joins など ('and such') to the quotative と, attaching to what someone said or thought: 行{い}きたくないなどと言{い}う ('says things like he doesn't want to go'). The など adds a dismissive or trivialising shade — the speaker treats the quoted content as unworthy or surprising. It pairs with verbs of saying/thinking (言{い}う・思{おも}う・考{かんが}える). The casual spoken contraction is なんて."
keySentence:
  jp: "彼{かれ}は「興味{きょうみ}がない」などと言{い}っていた。"
  en: "He was saying things like \"I'm not interested.\""
formation:
  - attaches_to: "Quoted word / phrase / clause + などと + verb of saying/thinking"
    form: "〜 などと + 言{い}う・思{おも}う"
    example: "無理{むり}だなどと / 知{し}らなかったなどと"
examples:

  - jp: "自分{じぶん}は悪{わる}くないなどと思{おも}っているのだろう。"
    en: "He probably thinks things like it's not his fault."

    en: "No one imagined it would actually succeed."
contrasts:
  - slug: "nante"
    label: "なんて"
    distinction: "なんて is the casual spoken contraction of などと; などと is the fuller, more neutral/written form, standard with verbs of saying and thinking."
  - slug: "to-quotative"
    label: "〜と (quotative)"
    distinction: "Plain と quotes neutrally ('says that ~'); などと adds など's dismissive 'things like ~' colouring to the quote, implying the speaker finds it trivial or hard to credit."
notes:
