equivalents:
  - "the instant ~ / the moment ~ — right as one action finished, a second (often unexpected) thing happened"
nuance: "たとたん (途端{とたん} = 'the very moment') marks an abrupt, often surprising sequence: the second event follows the first instantly and beyond the speaker's control. 立{た}ち上{あ}がったとたん、めまいがした ('the instant I stood up, I felt dizzy'). Both clauses are past and factual — it describes what *did* happen, so it can't be used for commands, requests, or intentions in the second clause."
keySentence:
  jp: "ドアを開{あ}けたとたん、猫{ねこ}が飛{と}び出{だ}した。"
  en: "The instant I opened the door, the cat shot out."

  - attaches_to: "Verb た-form + とたん(に)"
    form: "Vた + とたん(に)"
    example: "見{み}たとたん / 帰{かえ}ったとたん"
examples:

    en: "The moment I left the house, it started to rain."
  - jp: "電気{でんき}を消{け}したとたん、物音{ものおと}がした。"
    en: "The instant I turned off the light, there was a noise."
  - jp: "彼{かれ}は座{すわ}ったとたん、眠{ねむ}ってしまった。"
    en: "The moment he sat down, he fell asleep."
contrasts:
  - slug: "tara-sugu"
    label: "たらすぐ"
    distinction: "たらすぐ = 'as soon as', and the second clause can be planned (帰{かえ}ったらすぐ電話{でんわ}して, 'call as soon as you get back'); たとたん is a past, often *unexpected* happening — no commands or intentions allowed."
  - slug: "nari"
    label: "〜なり"
    distinction: "Both mark an instant sequence; なり is more literary and stresses one subject doing B straight off (見{み}るなり泣{な}き出{だ}した), while たとたん highlights a sudden, often surprising result and is the everyday choice."
notes:
  - text: "とたんに (with に) is interchangeable. The surprise/abruptness is core — a smoothly expected next step uses たら/と instead."
