  - "~ and ~ (among others) — joins nouns as a partial, representative list, implying more items exist"
nuance: "や lists a few examples and leaves the list open: 本{ほん}やノート means 'books and notebooks (and such)', not an exhaustive set. This is the key difference from と, which lists everything. や often closes with など ('…and so on') to make the open-endedness explicit. Nouns only — it doesn't join verbs or clauses."

contrasts:
  - slug: "to-2"
    label: "と (listing)"
    distinction: "と lists the items *exhaustively* ('A and B, and that's all'); や lists *representatively* ('A and B, among others'). 本{ほん}と雑誌{ざっし} = exactly those two; 本{ほん}や雑誌{ざっし} = those plus more."
  - slug: "toka"
    label: "とか"
    distinction: "Both are non-exhaustive, but とか is more casual and vaguer, and can also list actions, quotes, or soften a single item; や is noun-only and a touch more neutral."
