contrasts:
  - slug: "hoshii"
    label: "欲しい"
    distinction: "Both take が and express positive feeling, but 好{す}きだ = liking something that already exists ('I like dogs'), while 欲{ほ}しい = wanting to get or have something ('I want a dog': 犬{いぬ}が欲{ほ}しい). Fondness vs desire to obtain."

    en: "When I was a kid, I liked summer."
  - jp: "私{わたし}はあなたのことが好{す}きです。"
    en: "I like you."
notes:
  - text: "Intensify with 大好{だいす}きだ ('love / really like'). The opposite is 嫌{きら}いだ ('dislike'), also a な-adjective whose object takes が. With a person, のこと is often added: あなたのことが好{す}き sounds warmer and more natural than あなたが好{す}き."
