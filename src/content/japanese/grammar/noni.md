equivalents:
  - "although ~ / even though ~ — a contrast charged with surprise, frustration, or reproach"
nuance: "のに isn't a neutral 'but' — it carries the speaker's feeling that the result is unexpected or unfair ('…and yet…'). That emotional charge is what separates it from けど and が."
keySentence:
  jp: "たくさん勉強{べんきょう}したのに、試験{しけん}に落{お}ちた。"
  en: "Even though I studied a lot, I failed the exam."
formation:
  - attaches_to: "Clause (plain / adnominal)"
    form: "plain form + のに; N / na-adj + な + のに"
    example: "高{たか}いのに / 元気{げんき}なのに"
examples:
  - jp: "約束{やくそく}したのに、彼{かれ}は来{こ}なかった。"
    en: "Even though he promised, he didn't come."
  - jp: "もう春{はる}なのに、まだ寒{さむ}い。"
    en: "It's already spring, and yet it's still cold."
restrictions:
  - text: "のに reports an unexpected actual outcome, so the main clause can't be a command, request, or plan. For 'it's cold, so close the window' use から, not ×寒{さむ}いのに窓{まど}を閉{し}めて."
contrasts:
  - slug: "kedo"
    label: "けど"
    distinction: "けど/が are neutral contrasts; のに adds reproach or surprise. 雨{あめ}だけど行{い}く (just a fact) vs 雨{あめ}なのに行{い}く (going despite the rain — pointed)."
  - slug: "kara"
    label: "〜から"
    distinction: "Near-opposites: から gives the reason a result was expected; のに flags that the expected result failed to happen. 安{やす}いから買{か}う (so I buy) vs 安{やす}いのに買{か}わない (cheap, and yet I don't)."
notes:
  - text: "Left hanging at the end of a sentence, のに expresses regret on its own: 「言{い}ってくれればよかったのに」 ('you could've just told me…')."
