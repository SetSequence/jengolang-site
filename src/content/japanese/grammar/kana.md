equivalents:
  - "I wonder ~ / ~, I guess? — a casual question turned inward at oneself rather than asked of the listener"
nuance: "かな (か + ね) is musing out loud: you pose a question but don't really expect the other person to answer it. With a negative it tips into hoping — 来{こ}ないかな is less 'I wonder if it won't come' and more 'I kind of hope it comes'."
keySentence:
  jp: "明日{あした}は晴{は}れるかな。"
  en: "I wonder if it'll be sunny tomorrow."
formation:
  - attaches_to: "Plain form / noun / na-adjective (no だ on noun・na-adj)"
    form: "… plain + かな"
    example: "行{い}くかな / 元気{げんき}かな / 学生{がくせい}かな"
examples:
  - jp: "この服{ふく}、ちょっと派手{はで}かな。"
    en: "This outfit's a little flashy, maybe."
  - jp: "彼{かれ}、もう帰{かえ}ったかな。"
    en: "I wonder if he's already gone home."
  - jp: "早{はや}くバス来{こ}ないかな。"
    en: "I wish the bus would hurry up and come."
contrasts:
  - slug: "kashira"
    label: "かしら"
    distinction: "Same 'I wonder', different speaker: かな is gender-neutral and the everyday default; かしら is markedly feminine/old-fashioned."
  - slug: "no-darou-ka"
    label: "〜のだろうか"
    distinction: "Both wonder, but のだろうか is heavier and more written/introspective ('I wonder whether…'); かな is light, spoken musing."
notes:
  - text: "〜かな with a negative verb is the standard way to voice a wish: 上手{うま}くいかないかな ('I hope it goes well'). Said softly, often half to yourself."
