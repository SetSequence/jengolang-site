equivalents:
  - "actually ~ / but as it turned out ~ / well, the thing is ~ — prefaces a development that runs counter to what was expected"
nuance: "Sentence-opening それが flags that what follows defies the expectation just set up — your own, or the listener's. It's especially common as a reply that overturns an assumption: 「旅行{りょこう}は楽{たの}しかった?」「それが、ずっと雨{あめ}で…」 ('Was the trip fun?' 'Well, actually, it rained the whole time…'). It promises a twist, so it leans into spoken, conversational Japanese."
keySentence:
  jp: "うまくいくと思{おも}っていた。それが、土壇場{どたんば}で失敗{しっぱい}してしまった。"
  en: "I thought it would go well. But as it turned out, it fell apart at the last minute."
formation:
  - attaches_to: "Sentence-initial, often answering a question or following one's own setup"
    form: "[expectation]。それが、[the twist]。"
    example: "順調{じゅんちょう}だった。それが、急{きゅう}に…"
usageSetting: "Conversational; signals to the listener that an unexpected turn is coming. Frequently opens a reply that contradicts the question's assumption."
examples:
  - jp: "「もう終{お}わった?」「それが、まだ半分{はんぶん}も終{お}わってないんだ。」"
    en: "'Are you done?' 'Actually, I'm not even halfway through.'"
  - jp: "晴{は}れる予報{よほう}だった。それが、朝{あさ}から大雨{おおあめ}だ。"
    en: "The forecast said sunny. But here it is pouring since morning."
contrasts:
  - slug: "tokoro-ga"
    label: "ところが"
    distinction: "Both introduce a surprising actual result. ところが is a touch more written/narrative ('however, contrary to expectation'); それが is conversational and often opens a *reply* that upends what the other person assumed."
  - slug: "sore-nanoni"
    label: "それなのに"
    distinction: "それなのに = 'and yet', stressing a contradiction between two facts ('I studied hard, and yet I failed'); それが simply heralds an unexpected turn without framing it as unfair or contradictory."
notes:
  - text: "Don't confuse with それ + が as plain subject-marking (それが正解{せいかい}だ 'that's the right answer'). The connective それが stands alone at the head of a sentence, usually set off by a pause."
