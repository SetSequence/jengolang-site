restrictions:
  - text: "Used for other people's feelings, not your own — Japanese treats another person's inner state as something inferred from their behavior. Say 私{わたし}は怖{こわ}い for your own fear, not 私{わたし}は怖{こわ}がる."
contrasts:
  - slug: "tagaru"
    label: "〜たがる"
    distinction: "がる attaches to adjectives of feeling (怖{こわ}がる, ほしがる). たがる is the verb-desire version — V-stem + たがる ('show signs of wanting to do ~'), built from 〜たい."
  - slug: "hoshii"
    label: "〜ほしい"
    distinction: "ほしい states your own desire for a thing ('I want ~'). For someone else's desire you switch to ほしがる, the がる form, because you're reporting an observed wish rather than your own."
notes:
  - text: "Because がる makes a verb, it conjugates and takes ている for an ongoing display: 怖{こわ}がっている ('is (visibly) scared')."
