equivalents:
  - "over / through / across (a span) — marks the end of a range whose boundaries are approximate, not exact"
keySentence:
  jp: "今夜{こんや}から明日{あした}にかけて雨{あめ}が降{ふ}るでしょう。"
  en: "It will rain from tonight through tomorrow."
formation:
  - attaches_to: "Noun (time or place), usually paired with から"
    form: "(Nから) + Nにかけて"
    example: "春{はる}から夏{なつ}にかけて / 関東{かんとう}から東北{とうほく}にかけて"
examples:
  - jp: "九月{くがつ}から十月{じゅうがつ}にかけて台風{たいふう}が多{おお}い。"
    en: "There are many typhoons from September through October."
  - jp: "この地域{ちいき}は朝{あさ}から昼{ひる}にかけて風{かぜ}が強{つよ}い。"
    en: "In this area the wind is strong from morning into the afternoon."
  - jp: "首{くび}から肩{かた}にかけて痛{いた}みがある。"
    en: "There's pain spreading from my neck across to my shoulder."
restrictions:
  - text: "にかけて draws a vague, continuous spread, so it pairs with a single ongoing situation, not a one-moment event. Say 夜{よる}にかけて雨{あめ}が降{ふ}る for rain over a stretch of time, not 八時{はちじ}にかけて始{はじ}まる for a fixed start time (use 八時{はちじ}に)."
contrasts:
  - slug: "made"
    label: "まで"
    distinction: "まで marks a precise endpoint where something stops (五時{ごじ}まで = 'until exactly 5'). にかけて marks an approximate far edge of a range that the situation spreads across, without a sharp cut-off."
  - slug: "kara-ni-kakete"
    label: "〜から〜にかけて"
    distinction: "にかけて almost always appears as the second half of the から〜にかけて frame, which states both edges of the span; にかけて alone supplies just the ending edge."
notes:
  - text: "A separate idiom にかけては means 'when it comes to ~ (no one beats…)': 料理{りょうり}にかけては彼女{かのじょ}が一番{いちばん}だ ('when it comes to cooking, she's the best'). That is a different use from the time/place span here."
