# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Self-study Japanese learners past the very basics — they know hiragana, katakana, and foundational grammar, and are focused on expanding vocabulary and reading comprehension. They study on their own schedule, often in short sessions on a phone or browser tab. They have tried Anki (effective but decontextualized) and reading practice (contextual but passive), found each incomplete alone, and want something that bridges the two.

## Product Purpose

Jengo shows vocabulary inside real sentences so words are understood deeply, not just memorized. It is faster than reading alone (which rarely forces active recall) and more effective than isolated drilling (which strips words of meaning). The app lives at app.jengolang.com; jengolang.com markets it and hosts Japanese grammar and vocabulary content that funnels learners toward the app.

## Brand Personality

Direct. Earnest. Purposeful.

Voice is grounded in the builder's experience: two years living in Japan, stuck between reading and flashcards, built the thing he needed. Never corporate. No euphemisms. Copy says what the feature does.

## Anti-references

- **Memrise**: bright gamification, crowded UI, feels cheap and scatter-shot
- **Generic SaaS**: hero-metric cards, gradient text, glassmorphism, "the platform for teams who care about X"
- **Duolingo**: mascot-driven, gamified streaks, primary-color overload — treats learning like a game loop rather than a skill

Day One (dayoneapp.com) is the visual target: screenshot-led sections, direct feature language, secondary CTA placed before verbose content, overall restraint. Warmer than Day One — pull Jengo's green palette through.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the product, don't describe it.** Screenshots of the real Jengo UI carry the content weight. Body copy stays short and factual.
2. **Earn every word.** No restated headings, no intros that repeat the title, no copy that would fit any other app.
3. **Builder's honesty.** The creator note and product story are not marketing — they are true. Design should feel personal without feeling unpolished.
4. **Functional beauty.** Design serves the learner, not the portfolio. Every layout decision should feel obvious in hindsight.
5. **Calm confidence.** No urgency tricks, no FOMO copy. State the product plainly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Light mode only (the app has no dark mode yet). Mobile-first. Keep animations subtle; do not rely on motion to convey meaning.
